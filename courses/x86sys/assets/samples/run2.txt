1B. THE ORACLE: THE REFERENCE TABLES, PRINTED AND NOT MEASURED.
    Nothing in this section came out of this machine.  Every row is
    a bit position, and a bit position is exact -- which is why the
    harness can assert this section to the bit and asserts the rest
    of the file only as orderings and shapes.

1B1. CR0.  WHAT SETS A BIT, WHAT CLEARS IT, WHAT IT INVALIDATES.
     Readable only at CPL 0; section 3 measures that for itself.

  CR0 | 0    | PE  | protected-mode enable; RESET TO 1 by the CPU
  CR0 | 1    | MP  | monitor coprocessor; must be 1 on a 486+
  CR0 | 2    | EM  | emulation: 1 means NO x87 unit present
  CR0 | 3    | TS  | task switched: saves x87 state on a switch
  CR0 | 4    | ET  | extension type, RESERVED and ALWAYS 1 since the 486
  CR0 | 5    | NE  | numeric error internal to the FPU; 1 on a 486+
  CR0 | 6-15 | -   | reserved
  CR0 | 16   | WP  | write protect: honour read-only pages in CPL 0 TOO
  CR0 | 17   | -   | reserved
  CR0 | 18   | AM  | alignment mask: with EFLAGS.AC, check CPL 3 alignment
  CR0 | 19-28 | -  | reserved
  CR0 | 29   | NW  | not-write-through: caches may hold dirty lines
  CR0 | 30   | CD  | cache disable: the whole cache hierarchy off
  CR0 | 31   | PG  | paging enable; the MASTER bit of the register
  CR0 | 32-63 | - | reserved, written 0

  WRITING CR0 invalidates:  CR0.PG 0->1 flushes the TLB completely.
                              CR0.WP and CR0.AM change no TLB entry, so
                              the SDM requires NO invalidation for them
                              and this file has no instrument either.
  CR0.ET is the interesting row: a bit with no meaning and no way to
  read it, which survives because software once wrote 0 and then
  faulted.  A 'reserved, always 1' is a compatibility scar.

1B2. CR3.  THE PAGE-DIRECTORY BASE AND THE ADDRESS-SIZE BITS.

  CR3 | 0-11  | PCID   | address space tag, USED ONLY IF CR4.PCIDE=1
  CR3 | 12    | PWT    | page-table writes are write-through
  CR3 | 13    | PCD    | page-table writes bypass the cache
  CR3 | 14-47 | BASE   | physical address of the PML4, 4 KiB aligned
  CR3 | 48-51 | SEAM   | reserved, and MUST EQUAL bit 47 of BASE:
  CR3 |         |        | this is the sign-extension of the base and
  CR3 |         |        | it is the only thing in CR3 that is not a bit
  CR3 |         |        | you can set to a constant
  CR3 | 52-63 | ZERO   | must be zero in 4-level paging; with CR4.LA57=1
  CR3 |         |        | the 52-56 field grows a sign-extension role

  THE FOUR ADDRESS-SIZE BITS, which is what a compiler backend has to
  get right and what CR0.CR3 and CR4.LA57 select between:
    4-level paging   CR3[63:52] reserved-zero,  CR3[51:48] sign-extend
    5-level paging   CR3[63:58] reserved-zero,  CR3[57:52] sign-extend
  The address bits come from the CPUID, not from CR3, and section 2
  measures which of the two this machine reports.

  CR4 | 5    | PAE         | 36-bit physical; REQUIRED, always 1 in long mode
  CR4 | 6    | MCE         | machine-check exception enable
  CR4 | 7    | PGE         | translations may be shared between address spaces
  CR4 | 8    | PCE         | if 1, RDPMC is permitted at any CPL

  CR4 | 10   | OSXMMEXCPT  | unmasked SSE exceptions are delivered

  FIVE ROWS OF THAT TABLE DECIDE WHETHER THIS PROCESS CAN OBSERVE
  ANYTHING AT ALL, and every one of them is a bit in a register it
  cannot read:  CR4.UMIP decides whether SIDT and SGDT are legal at
  CPL 3.  CR4.PCE decides whether RDPMC is legal at CPL 3.
  CR4.TSD decides whether RDTSC is legal at CPL 3.  CR4.PCIDE and
  CR4.LA57 decide the shape of CR3.  Section 3 measures the
  CONSEQUENCES and section 2 measures what the kernel says the bits
  are.  What it CANNOT do is read CR4, and that is the limit.

1B4. DR0-DR7.  THE MASK, THE FORMULA, AND THE TWO SOURCES.

  DR0-DR3  | one LINEAR address each; four breakpoints, no more
  DR4,DR5  | aliases of DR6,DR7 when CR4.DE=0, #UD when CR4.DE=1
  DR6      | status: bits 0-3 B0-B3, 13 BD, 14 BS, 15 BT
  DR7      | control: the eight enable bits, the GD bit, and EIGHT
           | four-bit fields, one per breakpoint

  DR7 | 0     | L0      | local enable, breakpoint 0, cleared on a switch
  DR7 | 1     | G0      | global enable, breakpoint 0
  DR7 | 2     | L1      | local enable, breakpoint 1
  DR7 | 3     | G1      | global enable, breakpoint 1
  DR7 | 4     | L2      | local enable, breakpoint 2
  DR7 | 5     | G2      | global enable, breakpoint 2
  DR7 | 6     | L3      | local enable, breakpoint 3
  DR7 | 7     | G3      | global enable, breakpoint 3
  DR7 | 8     | LE      | local exact-breakpoint enable, ignored after the 486
  DR7 | 9     | GE      | global exact-breakpoint enable, ignored after the 486
  DR7 | 10    | ONE     | architecturally reserved to 1, the RESET value's bit
  DR7 | 11-12 | -       | reserved
  DR7 | 13    | GD      | general detect: #DB on any DR0-DR7 access
  DR7 | 14-15 | -       | reserved
  DR7 | 16-19 | FP0     | bits 17:16 R/W0, bits 19:18 LEN0
  DR7 | 20-23 | FP1     | bits 21:20 R/W1, bits 23:22 LEN1
  DR7 | 24-27 | FP2     | bits 25:24 R/W2, bits 27:26 LEN2
  DR7 | 28-31 | FP3     | bits 29:28 R/W3, bits 31:30 LEN3
  DR7 | 32-63 | -       | reserved, read as 0

  R/W | 00 | execute
  R/W | 01 | data write
  R/W | 10 | I/O read or write, ONLY defined while CR4.DE=1
  R/W | 11 | data read or write
  LEN | 00 | 1 byte
  LEN | 01 | 2 bytes
  LEN | 10 | 8 bytes, ONLY defined in 64-bit mode
  LEN | 11 | 4 bytes
  NOTE the order: LEN 10 is EIGHT bytes and LEN 11 is FOUR.  A table
  that says 10=4 reproduces the single most common DR7 bug there is.

  THE MASK A DEBUGGER MUST PROGRAM, and the formula, evaluated here
  rather than typed, so that a page cannot quote a stale constant:
    enable(i)  = (L_i ? 1 : 0) << (2*i) | (G_i ? 1 : 0) << (2*i+1)
    field(i)   = ((LEN_i & 3) << 2 | (RW_i & 3)) << (16 + 4*i)
    DR7        = 0x400 | GE<<9 | GD<<13 | sum enable(i) | sum field(i)

  DISAGREEMENT, PRINTED RATHER THAN SETTLED.  A third description,
  the Intel SDM as summarised in several secondary sources, places
  L2 and G2 at bits 8 and 9, LE and GE at 16 and 17, and starts the
  R/W0 field at bit 22 with FOUR-bit LEN fields.  Linux
  DR_LOCAL_ENABLE_MASK=0x55, DR_GLOBAL_ENABLE_MASK=0xAA,
  DR_CONTROL_SHIFT=16, DR_CONTROL_SIZE=4, DR7_FIXED_1=0x400 and
  QEMU's DR7_TYPE_SHIFT=16, DR7_LEN_SHIFT=18 all say the table
  above.  Two implementations that work on real silicon agree with
  each other.  This file CANNOT MEASURE WHICH ONE ITS OWN CPU
  IMPLEMENTS, because DR7 is unreadable from ring 3: section 3
  shows the MOV failing.  The instrument that would settle it is a
  one-line ptrace program running at CPL 0.

  DR7MASK | case | value | fields disjoint
  DR7MASK | four breakpoints, 4-byte, data write, global   | 0x00000000dddd06aa | yes
  DR7MASK | four breakpoints, 4-byte, data read/write, global | 0x00000000ffff06aa | yes
  DR7MASK | four breakpoints, 8-byte, data read/write, global | 0x00000000bbbb06aa | yes
  DR7MASK | one breakpoint, 1-byte, execute, LOCAL         | 0x0000000000000601 | yes
  DR7MASK | the mask for four 4-byte WRITE breakpoints | 0x00000000dddd06aa
  DR7MASK | derived from the formula above, not typed in
  DR7LOCAL | the same four, all LOCAL, no GD | 0x0000000055550455
  DR7RESET | the reset value: the fixed one and nothing else | 0x0000000000000400
1B5. THE SEGMENT SELECTOR, WHICH IS SIXTEEN BITS AND THREE FIELDS.

  SEL | 15:3 | index  | which descriptor in the GDT or LDT
  SEL | 2    | TI     | 0 = GDT, 1 = the LDT
  SEL | 1:0  | RPL    | requested privilege level, 0 to 3
  SEL | 0xffff | a NULL selector: index 8191, TI 1, RPL 3

  The four privilege numbers and the two comparisons:
    CPL   the current privilege level, from CS.RPL in long mode
    RPL   requested, from the low two bits of the selector loaded
    DPL   descriptor privilege level, bits 14:13 of the descriptor
    for a DATA or CODE segment:  max(CPL, RPL) <= DPL   or  #GP
    for a CALL GATE:             max(CPL, RPL) <= DPL   or  #GP(tss)
    for an INTERRUPT gate:       CPL <= DPL             or  #GP(vector)
  The third rule has no RPL in it because an interrupt gate is
  indexed by a VECTOR NUMBER and a vector number has no RPL field.
  Every textbook that quotes the first rule for 'gates' is quoting
  the CALL-GATE rule for something that is not a call gate.

1B6. THE FOUR ENTRIES, AND EVERY FLAG IN ONE OF THEM.

  IDX | PML4 | 9 bits | address bits 47:39
  IDX | PDPT | 9 bits | address bits 38:30
  IDX | PD   | 9 bits | address bits 29:21
  IDX | PT   | 9 bits | address bits 20:12
  IDX | OFF  | 12 bits | address bits 11:0, always added last
  9+9+9+9+12 = 48, and with LA57 there are five levels of 9.

  PTE | 0    | P       | present; 0 makes the entry INVALID and every
  PTE |      |         | access through it a #PF with error bit 0 clear
  PTE | 1    | RW      | writable; 0 with RW=1 above is the #PF case
  PTE | 2    | US      | accessible at CPL 3; 0 and CPL>0 is a #PF
  PTE | 3    | PWT     | write-through for this page
  PTE | 4    | PCD     | cache disabled for this page
  PTE | 5    | A       | accessed: SET BY HARDWARE on the first touch
  PTE | 6    | D       | dirty: SET BY HARDWARE on the first write
  PTE | 7    | PS      | page size: 0 = 4 KiB, 1 = 2 MiB, PD level ONLY
  PTE | 8    | G       | global: a translation usable by every address space
  PTE | 9-11 | -       | available to the operating system
  PTE | 12   | PAT     | at the PD level, the PAT bit for the whole 2 MiB
  PTE | 13-58 | -      | reserved, except bits 59-62 which hold PKRU
  PTE | 59-62 | PKRU   | protection key; ignored unless CR4.PKE or PKS
  PTE | 63   | NX      | no-execute; needs EFER.NXE, and a #PF without
  PTE |      |         | the instruction bit set if the fetch is denied
  PTE | 4-5 are the ONLY bits the HARDWARE sets.  Everything else in
  the entry is written by software, which is why a kernel's paging
  format is a choice and not a fact.

  The page-fault ERROR CODE, a fifth encoding with its own layout:
  ERR | 0   | P   | 0 = the page was not present
  ERR | 1   | W/R | 1 = the access was a WRITE, 0 = a read or a fetch
  ERR | 2   | U/S | 0 = the access was at CPL 0, 1 = at CPL 3
  ERR | 3   | RSVD| 1 = a reserved bit of the page-table entry was set
  ERR | 4   | I/D | 1 = the access was an INSTRUCTION FETCH
  ERR | 5   | PK  | 1 = a protection-key violation
  ERR | 6-31 | -  | must be zero
  Bits 1 and 4 are exactly the two a demand-paging system needs and
  section 4 shows that ring 3 cannot see either of them.

2B. THE TWO PSEUDO-DESCRIPTORS, READ BOTH WAYS ROUND ON PURPOSE.

  the ten bytes SIDT actually wrote, in memory order:
    00 00 00 00 ff ff ff ff ff ff
  SIDT | base 0xffffffff00000000 | limit 65535 | entries 4096 of 16 bytes
  SGDT | base 0xfffffffe00000000 | limit 65535 | entries 8192 of 8 bytes

  THE FIELD ORDER, and it is the whole of this sub-section:
    offset 0..7  BASE   first
    offset 8..9  LIMIT  second
  Read the documented-in-prose 'limit first, then base' way and:
    base 0xffffffffffff0000  limit 0
  NO FAULT, and no obviously impossible value either.  A limit of
  0 is INDISTINGUISHABLE from a kernel that installed an empty
  IDT, so the wrong reading is not merely wrong, it is
  UNFALSIFIABLE.  A tool written that way reports a plausible
  base in the middle of nowhere and never tells you.

  WHAT THE TWO BASES ARE, as arithmetic on the two numbers above:
    GDT base 0xfffffffe00000000 = 2^64 - 8589934592
    IDT base 0xffffffff00000000 = 2^64 - 4294967296
    IDT base - GDT base = 0x0000000100000000, i.e. 2^32 bytes apart
  Each table is EXACTLY 65535+1 bytes and nothing more: the GDT's
  8192 entries of 8 bytes and the IDT's 4096 entries of 16 bytes are
  both 2^16 bytes, and 2^16 is the most a 16-bit limit can describe.
  A FIRST DRAFT OF THIS SECTION said the two tables were ADJACENT,
  because it computed base+limit+1 and read the answer off a hex
  string by eye.  base+limit+1 is 0xfffffffe00010000 and the IDT base is
  0xffffffff00000000, and those are not equal: the gap between the two 4 GiB
  placements is 2^32 bytes and neither table is more than 2^16 long.
  The PLACEMENT is a choice this kernel made, the LENGTH is a
  consequence of the limit being 16 bits wide, and this file prints
  what it read and checks the arithmetic rather than asserting the
  addresses from a manual.

  AND NEITHER TABLE CAN BE READ.  Section 6 forks a child, takes
  the base out of SIDT, and reads ONE byte at it.

2C. THE OTHER REGISTERS A RING-3 PROCESS CAN READ, ALL OF THEM.

  XGETBV(0) | 0x0000000000000207 | x87 1 SSE 1 AVX 1 PKRU 1
  XGETBV(0) | XCR0 is 64 bits wide and XGETBV is UNPRIVILEGED, which
  is unusual: the INSTRUCTION that reads the register that decides
  which instructions you may execute is itself unprivileged.
  XSETBV, the write, is not: section 3 measures that.
  TR  | 0x0040 | index 8 TI 0 RPL 0
  TR  | and the `str %w0` form writes 16 bits and LEAVES THE UPPER
  TR  | BITS OF THE GENERAL REGISTER UNDEFINED, which gcc does not
  TR  | know, so the read is masked to 0xffff and the first version
  TR  | of this file printed TR as 0x35410040: a value that looks
  TR  | like an address and is sixteen bits of selector plus whatever
  TR  | the allocator had left there.  Sixteen bits is all of TR.
  SEG | cs 0x0033 ss 0x002b ds 0x0000 es 0x0000 fs 0x0000 gs 0x0000
  SEG | decoded as index<<3 | TI<<2 | RPL:
  SEG | cs | selector 0x0033 | index 6 TI 0 RPL 3 | CPL IS THE RPL OF CS IN LONG MODE
  SEG | ss | selector 0x002b | index 5 TI 0 RPL 3 | 
  SEG | ds | selector 0x0000 | index 0 TI 0 RPL 0 | 
  SEG | es | selector 0x0000 | index 0 TI 0 RPL 0 | 
  SEG | fs | selector 0x0000 | index 0 TI 0 RPL 0 | 
  SEG | gs | selector 0x0000 | index 0 TI 0 RPL 0 | 
  CS has RPL 3, so this process is at CPL 3, and SS has RPL 3 to
  match it.  DS, ES, FS and GS are all 0x0000 here: a NULL selector
  is perfectly legal to LOAD in 64-bit mode, and a NULL selector
  in a data segment no longer means the base is zero, because in
  long mode the base of DS and ES is ignored entirely.  Section 3
  shows that LOADING a non-zero selector into DS is what faults.

2D. `syscall` AND THE FOUR REGISTERS IT IS SUPPOSED TO TOUCH.

  The ISA destroys exactly TWO registers: RCX becomes the address of
  the following instruction and R11 becomes the RFLAGS that was
  saved on the way in.  A marker in each of the other thirteen, one
  syscall, read back immediately, in three batches:

  SYSCALL | register | marker in | marker out | verdict
  SYSCALL | rbx | 0xb000000000000010 | 0xb000000000000010 | SURVIVED
  SYSCALL | rdx | 0xb000000000000020 | 0xb000000000000020 | SURVIVED
  SYSCALL | rsi | 0xb000000000000030 | 0xb000000000000030 | SURVIVED
  SYSCALL | rdi | 0xb000000000000040 | 0xb000000000000040 | SURVIVED

  FOUR THINGS IN THAT TABLE THAT THE TEXTBOOK SAYS DIFFERENTLY,
  all of them settled here by running the instruction:
    1. SGDT and SIDT are NOT privileged.  CR4.UMIP can make them
       #GP at CPL>0, but whatever this kernel has UMIP set to, it is
       not blocking them, and that is a measurement of the EFFECT
       and not a reading of the bit.

       one every answer gets wrong, because the check for WRITING
       TR is CPL > IOPL and the check for READING it is nothing.

5.  THE CANONICAL BOUNDARY, ON FIVE WIDTHS INSTEAD OF THREE.

  A canonical 48-bit address has bit 47 EQUAL to bit 63.  The gap
  between the two halves is not an address: the CPU rejects it while
  it is still generating the effective address, before the MMU is
  consulted, so there is no page-table entry to look up and no
  present bit to clear.  That is why it is not a page fault.

  change over at the same offset, and that is not noise: an access
  that STARTS below 2^47 still fails if it REACHES 2^47, because the
  CPU validates the whole byte range of the operand and not its
  first byte.  A 1-byte read therefore gets right up to the line.

  THE UPPER HALF, AND THE CONSTANT THAT WAS WRONG IN THE DRAFT.

  A first version of this section asserted that the upper canonical
  half began at 0xffffffff80000000.  It does not, and 0xffffffff80000000
  is not even its lowest address: it is 2^31 bytes INSIDE the half.
  Nobody who wrote that sentence was wrong about the half being
  there, and everybody who has read it has believed the base is at the
  2^31 mark, which is a different number and a different claim.  The
  table below is the answer, and only four of the nine rows are
  addresses at all:

  UPPER | address | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | reading
  UPPER | 0x00007fffffffffff | 1 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | canonical, not mapped
  UPPER | 0x0000800000000000 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | NOT AN ADDRESS
  UPPER | 0x7fffffffffffffff | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | NOT AN ADDRESS
  UPPER | 0x8000000000000000 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | NOT AN ADDRESS
  UPPER | 0xc000000000000000 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | 128 | NOT AN ADDRESS
  UPPER | 0xffffff0000000000 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | canonical, not mapped
  UPPER | 0xffffffff00000000 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | canonical, not mapped
  UPPER | 0xffffffff80000000 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | canonical, not mapped
  UPPER | 0xffffffffffffffff | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | canonical, not mapped

  0xffffffff80000000 is NOT an address on this machine, and a
  second version of this section discovered that by bisecting for the
  boundary instead of assuming it:
  UPPER | and bisecting for it, once per access width, the first
  UPPER | address of the upper half that is an address AT ALL:

  BISEL | width | first address of the upper half | 2^64 - 2^47 | holds
  BISEL | 1-byte | 0xffff800000000000                 | 0xffff800000000000 | YES
  BISEL | 2-byte | 0xffff800000000000                 | 0xffff800000000000 | YES
  BISEL | 4-byte | 0xffff800000000000                 | 0xffff800000000000 | YES
  BISEL | 8-byte | 0xffff800000000000                 | 0xffff800000000000 | YES
  BISEL | 16-byte | 0xffff800000000000                 | 0xffff800000000000 | YES
  BISEL | 2^64 - 2^47 on 5 of 5 widths
  UPPER | and the SAME address for all five, which is the other half
  UPPER | of the same result: the LOWER edge of a canonical half is
  UPPER | one address and does not depend on the access width, while
  UPPER | the UPPER edge does, because that is the edge the rule is
  UPPER | about.  0xffffffff80000000 is the address people quote,
  UPPER | and it is 2^31 bytes into the hole.

  ONE ASYMMETRY, MEASURED AND NOT EXPLAINED, because this file
  would rather print a thing it cannot account for than a story
  about it.  A SIXTEEN-BYTE read at 0xffffffffffffffff, whose range
  runs off the end of the address space and wraps, comes back as a
  real address (si_code 1) rather than as a rejection:
  WRAP | 16-byte read at 0xffffffffffffffff | si_code 1
  WRAP | 16-byte read at 0xfffffffffffffff0 | si_code 1
  WRAP | 8-byte read at 0xffffffffffffffff    | si_code 1
  WRAP | So the LOW half's end is range-checked, on five widths, and
  WRAP | the TOP of the space is not observed to be.  This file does
  WRAP | not say why, because the two candidate explanations -- that
  WRAP | the implementation compares the offset within the half and
  WRAP | the high half's offset arithmetic cannot overflow, or that
  WRAP | the top of the space is simply never used and the hardware
  WRAP | never agreed to define it -- need a CPUID bit or a manual
  WRAP | sentence this process has no way to check.  Named as an
  WRAP | OPEN QUESTION, which is a different thing from being wrong.

6.  WHAT IS ABSENT, AND EVERY ABSENCE PROVED WITH A FORK.

  6A. THE IDT AND THE GDT ARE AT A KNOWN ADDRESS AND ARE NOT
      MAPPED INTO THIS PROCESS.

  ABSENT | what | si_code | si_addr | si_addr equals
  ABSENT | read one byte of the IDT | 1 | 0xffffffff00000000 | the IDT base SIDT printed
  ABSENT | read one byte of the GDT | 1 | 0xfffffffe00000000 | the GDT base SGDT printed
  ABSENT | both are si_code 1, SEGV_MAPERR, which is the shape of
  ABSENT | a MAPPING failure and not the shape of a #GP.  So the
  ABSENT | register is readable, the address is readable, and the
  ABSENT | table is not, and the reason is that no user mapping
  ABSENT | covers it.  That is a PAGE-TABLE fact, not a privilege
  ABSENT | fact, and it is a better story than 'the IDT is
  ABSENT | privileged' because it predicts the si_addr.

      all three refused, and none of them is a statement about the

  ABSENT | perf_event_open, a NULL attr | -1 | errno 13 | EACCES
  ABSENT | open /dev/cpu/0/msr | DENIED | errno 13 | EACCES: root only
  ABSENT | and yet CPUID.80000008.EBX bit 6 core-PMU = 1 and
  ABSENT | bit 15 L3-PMU = 1, so the METAL HAS PERFORMANCE
  ABSENT | MONITORS and this process is not allowed to read them.
  ABSENT | A first draft of this course said 'this machine has
  ABSENT | no PMU'.  The right sentence is about ACCESS, and it
  ABSENT | is a better one because it is falsifiable: it says
  ABSENT | which permission is missing, and a page that cannot
  ABSENT | name the missing permission is not making a claim.

  6C. THERE IS NO AVX-512, WHICH IS A DIFFERENT KIND OF ABSENCE,
      because it is a FEATURE BIT rather than a permission.

  ABSENT | CPUID.7.0:EBX AVX512F 0 DQ 0 CD 0 BW 0 VL 0 | ALL ZERO
  ABSENT | XCR0 = 0x0000000000000207, and the AVX-512 STATE bits of XCR0
  ABSENT | (5 opmask, 6 zmm_hi256, 7 hi16_zmm) are all 0, so even
  ABSENT | the state is not merely unsupported, it is not enabled.
  ABSENT | A feature bit is a FACT ABOUT THE CPU that any process
  ABSENT | can read with an unprivileged instruction, which is the
  ABSENT | opposite of a permission: the absence of AVX-512 is
  ABSENT | DECLARED BY THE HARDWARE, and the absence of the PMU is
  ABSENT | imposed by software.  Two absences, two different kinds.

7.  RETRACTIONS, AND LIMITS, PRINTED RATHER THAN FOOTNOTED.

   R1. "A ring-3 process cannot read the IDT because the IDT is
       privileged."  RETRACTED AND REPLACED.  The REGISTER is
       readable: SIDT returns base and limit from ring 3, and section
       2 prints them.  What cannot be read is one byte of the TABLE,
       and the reason is not privilege: the fault is si_code 1,
       SEGV_MAPERR, with si_addr EXACTLY the base SIDT printed.  A
       #GP would have been si_code 128 and si_addr 0, which is what
       every privileged instruction in section 3 produced.  The right
       sentence is a PAGE-TABLE sentence, and it predicts the address
       the first sentence could not.

   R2. "The 16-byte arm of the canonical sweep measures the
       canonical boundary."  RETRACTED.  It measured an ALIGNMENT
       requirement, because gcc compiled the __int128 load to
       `movdqa`, and the sweep then reported si_code 128 for every
       unaligned address at every offset, which looks like a rule and
       is not one.  Written in inline `movdqu` the same sweep gives
       2^47 - 15, the value the rule predicts, and section 5 prints
       BOTH arms side by side so that the retraction has numbers.
       A compiler will strengthen an instruction you asked for by name
       in no way at all, and a harness that is not read cannot notice.

   R3. "One inline-asm block can read all fourteen of the registers
       a syscall might destroy."  RETRACTED.  It died with SIGBUS,
       because gcc was holding the ADDRESS of one of the fourteen
       output slots in a register the body had already overwritten.
       There are fewer than fourteen allocatable registers once rax
       and rcx are clobbered, so the block cannot be written.  It is
       now three blocks of six, six and one, and each register it
       overwrites is NAMED in that block's clobber list.

   R4. "`syscall` destroys fifteen registers."  NEVER TRUE, AND IT
       WAS THIS FILE'S OWN OUTPUT FOR ONE RUN.  The first version of
       the fourteen-register table, which had the clobber-list bug
       from R3, reported all fifteen clobbered -- and it was the
       HARNESS talking, not the CPU.  Fixed, twelve of the thirteen
       markers come back and only R11 does not, which is what the
       ISA promises and what the x86-64 syscall ABI documents.  A
       wrong result that agrees with a plausible story is the hardest
       kind to catch, and this one agreed with a story the author had
       already written down.

   R5. "LAM is reported in CPUID leaf 7 subleaf 0."  WRONG, AND IT
       HAD THE RIGHT ANSWER.  LAM is leaf 7 SUBLEAF 1, EAX bit 26.
       A first version read subleaf 0 ECX bit 26 -- the same number,
       a different register, a different subleaf -- got zero, and
       reported LAM as unsupported with a confident sentence.  It
       happened to be right and it was still wrong.

   R6. "The canonical boundary is the address 2^47."  NOT AS A
       STATEMENT ABOUT AN ACCESS.  It is 2^47 MINUS (size - 1),
       because the CPU validates the whole byte range of the operand
       and not its first byte.  The privilege course established this
       on THREE widths and this file re-derives it on FIVE, adding
       the 2-byte and 16-byte arms.  The 2-byte one is the arm that
       separates the rule from a constant.

   R7. "This machine has no performance monitoring hardware."
       RETRACTED.  CPUID.80000008.EBX bit 6 and bit 15 say the core
       and L3 performance monitors ARE present, in the same breath in
       which RDPMC is not permitted, perf_event_open returns EACCES,
       and /dev/cpu/0/msr is root-only.  What is absent is ACCESS.
       The retraction matters because the two sentences have
       different consequences: one says the hardware cannot be used
       and the other says a permission has to be changed.

   R8. "si_addr is the address the CPU faulted on."  NOT A CLAIM
       THIS FILE CAN MAKE.  For a #GP there is no address and the
       kernel reports 0; for a page fault the address is exact.  Both
       are measured in sections 3, 4 and 5, and the field that tells
       them apart is si_code, not si_addr.  A handler that reads
       si_addr before si_code concludes a #GP dereferenced NULL.

   R9. "Writing a PROT_READ page and touching a PROT_NONE page are
       different protections, so they are distinguishable."  THEY
       ARE NOT, from ring 3.  Both are si_code 2 with an si_addr.
       Only the null dereference, si_code 1, is distinguishable, and
       it is distinguished by MAPPED-ness rather than by protection.
       The page-fault error code has five other bits, of which a
       process sees one.

  R10. "The high bit of a /proc/self/pagemap word is the PTE's
       no-execute bit."  WRONG, AND IT REPORTED NX=1 FOR A PAGE
       THAT HAD NEVER BEEN WRITTEN.  Bit 63 of the PAGEMAP word is
       PM_PRESENT, which is the PTE's PRESENT bit; NX is bit 63 OF
       THE PTE and pagemap exposes no field for it.  What the file
       actually measured is that an untouched mapping has no entry at
       all, which is demand paging seen from the inside.

  LIMITS -- what this artifact cannot tell you, printed not footnoted,
  and each one names the instrument that would settle it:
    * ANY CYCLE COUNT.  There is no counter this process can open:
      RDPMC is a #GP, perf_event_open is EACCES, and the kernel
      reports perf_event_paranoid above.  Every duration in this file
      is a DURATION, and a duration bounds a count without measuring
      it.  Settled by perf_event_open with a paranoid setting below 4
      and a PMU passthrough from the host.
    * WHETHER CR0 TO CR4 HOLD THE VALUES THE KERNEL REPORTS.
      Section 2 prints arch_prctl's SMEP/SMAP/SHSTK bits and section
      3 measures that every read of CR0-CR4 and CR0-CR8 is a fault.
      A consequence is not a reading.  Settled by a CPL 0 program.
    * WHETHER SMEP AND SMAP ARE ON.  A report, not a measurement:
      a process cannot exec a supervisor page to find out, because
      there are none.  Settled by a CPL 0 program that reads CR4.
    * THE IA32_STAR, IA32_LSTAR AND IA32_SFMASK VALUES.  RDMSR is a
      fault and /dev/cpu/0/msr is root-only, both measured.  Settled
      by a CPL 0 program, or by reading the entry code in the vDSO.
    * THE CLASS OF ANY EXCEPTION.  Fault, trap and abort are read
      from a manual in section 4, and marked QUOTED on every row.
      Deciding one needs the saved RIP, which the kernel does not
      put in the siginfo.  Settled by a kernel-side trap handler.
    * THE ERROR CODE THE CPU PUSHED.  si_code is the kernel's
      translation of it, and section 4 measures that one of the five
      meaningful bits survives.  Settled by reading orig_ax in pt_regs.
    * WHICH DR7 LAYOUT THIS CPU IMPLEMENTS.  Section 1B prints two
      descriptions that disagree and says so; section 3 measures
      that DR7 cannot be read from ring 3.  Settled by one ptrace
      PTRACE_PEEKUSER at DR7, at CPL 0, in five lines.
    * THE PS BIT OF A REAL 2 MiB MAPPING.  MAP_HUGETLB was tried and
      returned ENOMEM on this machine, and no transparent huge page
      collapsed during the run, so the PS=1 row of the entry table in
      section 1B is QUOTED and the 4 KiB path is the only one
      MEASURED.  Settled by a machine with huge pages configured.
    * AArch64, RISC-V, or any other architecture.  Not run, not
      quoted, and deliberately absent: the comparison lives in the
      neutral courses and this one is the x86-64 reference.
    * A SECOND RUN'S ADDRESSES.  Every address in this file moves
      with ASLR and with the heap, so a page that quotes one quotes
      it as a RELATION and the relation is the measurement.

  What this artifact can support: an inventory of what a ring-3
  process can and cannot learn about the machine it is running on,
  with every entry of the CANNOT column a signal number rather than a
  quotation, and every entry of the CAN column a bit pattern read out
  of a register on this CPU.
  What it cannot support: any claim about a control register's value,
  any cycle count, any exception's class, and any statement about a
  second machine.  Four of those five are properties of the
  instrument, not of the subject.
